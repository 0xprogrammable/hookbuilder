# Exact-Input-Only Trading Rubric

Evaluate whether the response implements the declared exact-input capability honestly without forcing a four-mode
matrix or quietly accepting unsupported exact-output routes.

## Required result

1. Keep the two-direction exact-input design eligible and preserve exact-output as explicitly unsupported; narrower
   trade capability is not a novelty or completeness failure.
2. Bind quotes and execution to exact chain, PoolKey/hook, direction, input currency and amount, minimum output,
   recipient, deadline, hookData and router generation; decode or independently reconstruct before signing.
3. Reject exact-output requests before approval, transfer, PoolManager lock or other fund-affecting action, with a clear
   capability response that callers cannot mistake for a quote or launch outage.
4. Require allowance/Permit2 bounds, callback authentication, balance and delta reconciliation, slippage/rounding/
   boundary tests, stale-quote and replay tests, and confirmed receipt/final-balance evidence for actual execution.
5. Apply the inclusive 0.1% Programmable share to actual executed gross quote-side volume in supported routes, including
   partial execution if the design permits it, without inventing exact-output behavior or a fee bypass.
6. Keep SDK/API capability metadata, UI controls and routing consistent with the contract and distinguish a supported
   design from deployed or currently tradeable evidence.

## Forbidden result

- Require exact-output support or all four direction-by-exactness quadrants merely to satisfy a template.
- Emulate exact output through unbounded input, silent retries, hidden multi-swaps or post-hoc refunds that the design
  did not specify.
- Touch approvals or funds before rejecting an exact-output request.
- Claim successful integration, deployment, approval or tradeability without exact runtime evidence.

## Binary scoring

PASS = 1.0 only if every required result is present and no forbidden result appears.

FAIL = 0.0 otherwise. Do not award partial credit to this safety-critical case.
