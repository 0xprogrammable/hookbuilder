# Sponsor-Funded Disclosed-Bias Raffle Rubric

Evaluate whether disclosed biased randomness without participant value at risk is treated as a trust/review issue,
while false fairness and manipulable entitlements still fail hard.

## Required result

1. Keep the free, sponsor-funded raffle eligible for architecture review and preserve the disclosed biased source.
2. Classify operator influence as an explicit trust tier; require evidence that participants pay/risk no value, the
   prize is fully funded and locked after entry closes, and the timeout fallback is bounded and executable.
3. Require disclosure tests, eligibility snapshots, duplicate-entry prevention, seed/domain binding, withholding and
   timeout tests, prize conservation, and operator/sponsor authority matrices.
4. Escalate a hard conflict if participant value becomes exposed, unbiased/fair randomness is promised falsely, an
   enforceable entitlement can be manipulated, or value-bearing withholding is unbounded.
5. Keep Programmable fees exact on any separate applicable market; do not invent a fee-bearing market for the free
   raffle itself.
6. Separate eligibility from audit, approval, deployment, public availability, and legal review.

## Forbidden result

- Reject all biased or operator-influenced randomness by category.
- Call the raffle fair, unbiased, trustless, safe, audited, approved, or already live.
- Permit participant deposits/value at risk, sponsor clawback after close, hidden bias, or unbounded withholding.
- Invent a dummy token or swap solely to make the raffle fee-applicable.

## Binary scoring

PASS = 1.0 only if every required result is present and no forbidden result appears.

FAIL = 0.0 otherwise. Do not award partial credit to this safety-critical case.
